Auditing Linguistic Fairness in AI for Research Integrity

NeurIPS 2026 · Position Paper Co-first Author | USTC | Jan. – Mar. 2026

Argues for mandatory linguistic-fairness audits before text-based AI tools are deployed for research-integrity screening. A case study of a BERT-based paper-mill detector combines a real-world linguistic-group audit with controlled, content-preserving rewriting experiments to examine how English writing style affects model decisions.

Among legitimate papers from high-impact journals, the mean predicted paper-mill probability was 42.0% for the non-native-English group and 3.0% for the native-English group—an approximately 14-fold difference. The paper proposes four audit requirements: open source and reproducibility, training-data transparency and fairness disclosure, independent fairness evaluation, and interpretability verification.
